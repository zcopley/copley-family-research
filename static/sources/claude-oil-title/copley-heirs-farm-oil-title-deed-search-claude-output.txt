D. AAPG Bulletin, June 1927

Title: "The Copley Oil Pool of West Virginia," David B. Reger
Publication: Bulletin of the American Association of Petroleum Geologists,
volume 11, issue 6, June 1927
URL:
https://archive.org/details/sim_american-association-of-petroleum-geologists-aapg_1927-06_11_6/page/595/mode/1up

Key result: the article says the Copley pool opened on 13 September 1900 with
the South Penn Oil Company's Michael Copley Heirs well No. 1. Table II is
titled "Production Record of Michael Copley Heirs 128-Acre Lease, as Furnished
by South Penn Company." It lists eight wells, including No. 1 completed
13 September 1900 with reported initial production of 5,000 barrels in the
first 24 hours. The article says the company record may understate the earliest
flow, which local employees estimated at 10,000-12,000 barrels.

The source confirms the formal company designation "Michael Copley Heirs
128-Acre Lease." It does not identify the lease signers or allocate ownership.

2. Named Owners or Heirs

The will directly names John Copley and daughters Anna, Bridget, Margaret, and
Sarah in the core estate arrangement. Thomas and two additional difficult
recipient names receive cash bequests. Existing project person pages identify
the likely recipients as Catherine Hannon and Mary Quinn, but the exact will
readings remain subject to clearer-image verification.

The oil publications identify the property collectively as the Michael Copley
Heirs 128-Acre Lease or Copley heirs' farm. They do not directly identify each
lease signer. The report's proposed list of lease parties is an inference and
should not be treated as proven until the recorded lease or related deed is
inspected.

3. Interpretation

The published oil sources establish that South Penn Oil Company operated a
128-acre property formally designated the "Michael Copley Heirs" lease and
that its No. 1 well opened the Copley pool on 13 September 1900. This is
stronger than a generic family-name association.

The research does not yet establish the recorded lease's book/page, signers,
ownership shares, royalty terms, or whether the property was partitioned.

The uninspected 1900 FamilySearch legal-instrument result is a promising lead,
but its relationship string and record type require original-image review.
