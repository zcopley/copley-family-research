RQ-M1 INTERPRETATION

Does the tax-list evidence support an older Luke Murry in Lewis County before 1854?

No. After checking every available M-section page across all three Lewis County image sets from 1817 through 1855, no "Luke Murray" / "Luke Murry" / "Lucas Murray" ever appears as a taxpayer. If an older Luke existed - old enough to father Patrick before the 1854 marriage year - he would have been taxpaying in the 1830s-1840s at the latest. The complete absence of any Luke Murry across the entire 1817-1855 span is strong negative evidence. The only Murry family members who do appear are female ("Murry Eliza Jane," 1853) and two men named Patrick and James (both 1854).

Does it support Patrick and younger Luke arriving only after 1850?

Partly, yes. Patrick Murry first appears in Lewis County in 1854. "Murry Eliza Jane" appears in 1853, suggesting the Murry name entered Lewis County no earlier than ca. 1852-1853. The complete absence before 1853 across 36 years of annual lists confirms this is a recent family arrival. The 1854 cluster (Patrick + James Murray + the Loghan family in the same district) looks like a group migration event.

What does this mean for the "Luke Murray who married Anne Loghan" in 1854?

The 1854 Luke Murray who married Anne Loghan is almost certainly not Patrick Murry's father, for two reasons: (1) Patrick himself is already a taxable adult in 1854, making him a near-contemporary of the groom; and (2) no older Luke Murry ever appears in the tax lists. The "Murray James" who also appears in 1854 may be another brother. The family group (Patrick + James + possibly "Eliza Jane" as a widowed mother or sister) may have migrated to Lewis County ca. 1852-1853, with Luke coming at roughly the same time or slightly earlier (hence no tax record for him before the year of his marriage).

What exact next record should be checked after this?

The highest-priority follow-up is the Lewis County deed and court records for 1852-1860, specifically:
1. The 1854 Lewis County marriage register to confirm the full names Luke Murray / Anne Loghan - and whether any witness or bondsman is named Patrick, James, or "Murry."
2. The 1860 Lewis County federal census (Lewis County, WV) - search for Luke Murray, Patrick Murray, and James Murray households. If they are brothers, they may appear as near neighbors.
3. The WV county deed indexes for Lewis County ca. 1852-1860 for any Murray land purchase (would fix the immigration date precisely).
4. The 1855-1856 and 1857-1860 tax lists (i3811885) should be browsed for "Luke Murry" - this remains the only gap in the current survey.
