TAX-LIST 1855-1860 GAP CHECK SCOPE

Collection / website: Virginia, U.S., Personal Property Tax Records, 1782-1900 - Lewis County. Ancestry.com. Collection 62774.

Image sets checked:
- i3811884 (646 images total): covers Lewis County 1851-1856 per the Library of Virginia camera-operator declaration at viewer image 641.
- i3811885 (543 images total): covers Lewis County 1857-1860, confirmed by the 1857 year card at image 18, the 1858 cover at image 201, the 1859 cover "Property Book 2nd Dist. 1859" at image 331, and the 1860 Auditor's Office page at image 401; the "End of Records or Volume" card appears at image 537.

Years checked: 1855, 1856, 1857, 1858, 1859, 1860.

Access or image problems:
- Throughout both sets, each physical tax-list page (name column on the left) and its corresponding "PROOF" aggregate column (right side) appear to have been filmed as separate images and digitized in a mixed interleaved sequence. This causes long runs of PROOF pages that interrupt the name-list sequence and initially obscured the location of M-section name pages.
- The year digit in the PROOF page header "for the year 185_" is cut off at the right edge on almost every PROOF page in i3811884; year identification had to rely on cover/label images and the camera-operator declaration.
- The "Murty / Murtz" entry in the 1858 E. Slesher district (image 304, line 10) is in a degraded faded section; exact given-name initial uncertain.
- Line 26 (Murrow Hudson) on image 436 (1860) had its tax columns partially obscured by the Ancestry image-counter overlay during zoomed reads; tithable count of 1 was confirmed, full tax value not recoverable without a fresh unobscured capture.
- The 1860 Elijah Flesher 1st District book (images 535+) has essentially no name-list pages visible within the remaining reel frames before "End of Records."

Zoom / tile method used: Ancestry browser viewer; zoom-in/zoom-out buttons used; systematic zoom region captures of the name column (left ~165-380 px) and header rows; navigation by direct URL substitution of the zero-padded image suffix (i3811885-00NNN); cross-checked by PROOF page year confirmations and cover-label images.

RQ-M1 INTERPRETATION

Does this change the post-1850 arrival / first-tax-appearance model?

No - it reinforces it. The prior work confirmed James Murray and Patrick Murry in 1854. The 1855 and most of the 1856 M-sections are unavailable, so no earlier appearance can be ruled in or out from those years. The 1857 M-section confirms both James and Patrick continuing without a gap. The 1854 appearance remains the earliest confirmed tax-list entry in this collection for these individuals.

New information: A third Murray male appears in 1857 - Murray Hudson - who does not appear in the confirmed 1854 sequence. Hudson is present again in 1859 as Murrow Hudson and in 1860 as Murrow Hudson. He either arrived between 1854 and 1857 or was below tithable age in 1854. A fourth - Murray Edwards - appears only in 1857 and is absent from all checked later years; his status is unresolved. Murray William (1858, E. Slesher district) is in a geographically distinct district from the Geo. J. Marsh Murrays and may represent a different Murray household.

Does this add or weaken evidence for an older Luke / Margaret Murry parent couple in Lewis County?

Neither adds nor weakens directly. No Luke, Lucas, or Margaret Murray/Murry has surfaced in any checked M-section from 1817-1860. The 1857-1860 data introduces Hudson and Edwards as new Murray given names alongside James and Patrick. The coexistence of four distinct Murray males in 1857, all in the same Geo. J. Marsh district, is consistent with a family group, but the tax lists alone do not establish the relationship. "Murray Hudson" is an unusual name combination; Hudson could be a maternal surname used as a given name.

Does this affect the 1854 James / Patrick interpretation?

Confirms and extends. James Murray and Patrick Murry are verified in 1854 and again in 1857 under the Geo. J. Marsh district. James Murray continues into 1860. Patrick Murray is not found in 1858-1860 in the checked images, but the absence is not conclusive because M-section coverage is partial for 1858-1860.
