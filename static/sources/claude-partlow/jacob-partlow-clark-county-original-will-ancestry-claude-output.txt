Close transcript / abstract:
Estate of Jacob Partlow, deceased.
Jacob Partlow of Clark County, Illinois, being old but of sound mind and memory, made his will. First, funeral expenses and just debts were to be paid. Second, after debts and expenses, he gave "Polly my wife" all estate left, to be hers as long as she lived. Third, at her death, her funeral expenses and just debts were to be paid, and then the will states that Jacob had given all lawful heirs fifty dollars apiece. William was to have all both personal and real estate, except Polly Ritenhouse / Ridenhouse was to have a cow and bed. Fourth, Jacob appointed William W. Willison executor. The will was signed by Jacob's mark on 3 Sep 1867.

Probate attestation:
Witnesses Jacob Geffs and Roger S. Davis swore in open court on 26 May 1868 that the document was Jacob Partlow's last will and testament, that they signed as attesting witnesses at his request and in his presence, and that he was of full age, sound mind and memory, and under no constraint. A. B. Briscoe / Briscor[e], County Clerk.

Answers:
1. The original will does not name John H. / John Halleck Partlow.
2. It names only unnamed heirs collectively: "all my lawful heirs." It states those heirs had already received fifty dollars apiece.
3. It confirms wife Polly, not Mary. Polly is probably Mary / Polly Abrams by correlation, but the will itself says Polly.
4. It names William only. It does not identify him as William Franklin Partlow or as a son.
5. Polly Ritenhouse / Ridenhouse is named as recipient of a cow and bed, but her relationship is not stated.
6. Witnesses Jacob Geffs and Roger S. Davis and executor William W. Willison do not suggest kinship from the will text alone. They should be researched as neighbors / associates.

Evidence assessment:
The will-book entry is an official court copy and is sufficient to confirm Jacob Partlow's will text and probate date. It does not name John H. Partlow, does not list all heirs, and gives no parentage or birthplace clue. It leaves the key proof target in the accompanying probate packet: executor bond, letters testamentary, inventory, sale bill, settlement, receipts, or distribution papers.
