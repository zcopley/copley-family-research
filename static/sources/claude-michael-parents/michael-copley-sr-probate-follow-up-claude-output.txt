8. "Copley Heirs Farm" / "Copley Heirs Well No. 1" -- Oil Industry References (Collateral Evidence)

Sources found via web search:
- Petroleum Review (Internet Archive), mentioning "Copley heirs' farm, located on Sand Fork, Lewis county" as the site of a major oil well
- Bradford Era, Dec. 18, 1900, p. 3: "Copley heirs no. 1 ... John Copley no. 3" -- listing oil well production figures by well name
- Picturing West Virginia (WV and Regional History Center, WVU): panoramic photograph captioned "Copley Heirs Well No. 1 in Sand Fork District, Lewis County"
- WVU Archives: James Pietro Papers contain "Copley Heirs Well No. 1, Sand Fork District, Lewis County, WV; 1900/09/23"
- AAPG Bulletin: "The Copley Oil Pool of West Virginia" -- "The Copley pool is located in Lewis and Gilmer counties, West Virginia, on Sand Fork of Little Kanawha River, near the village of Copley"

Abstract of key finding: The "Copley Heirs Farm" at Sand Fork was a major oil-producing property in 1900-1901. The Bradford Era newspaper production listing names both "Copley heirs no. 1" and "John Copley no. 3" as distinct oil wells, implying that John Copley was an identified individual heir who had his own well on what may have been a partition of estate land. This suggests the Copley estate land was not sold outright but was divided among heirs or operated jointly, consistent with oil-era estate practice in WV.
