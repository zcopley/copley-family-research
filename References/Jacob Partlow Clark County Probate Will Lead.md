## Close Abstract

Jacob Partlow described himself as old but of sound mind and memory. He directed payment of funeral expenses and debts. He then gave **Polly my wife** all remaining estate for her life. After Polly's death and payment of her funeral expenses and debts, the will states that Jacob had given all his lawful heirs **fifty dollars apiece**. The residue of both personal and real estate was to go to **William**, except **Polly Ritenhouse / Ridenhouse** was to receive **a cow and bed**. Jacob appointed **William W. Willison** executor.

The probate attestation says witnesses Jacob Geffs and Roger S. Davis subscribed as attesting witnesses at Jacob's request and in his presence on 3 Sep 1867. They swore in open court on 26 May 1868 that Jacob was of full age, sound mind and memory, and under no constraint.

## Key Findings

### John H. / John Halleck Partlow

The original will-book entry does **not** name John H. Partlow or John Halleck Partlow. The only individually named people in the will are:

- Polly, Jacob's wife.
- William, residuary beneficiary.
- Polly Ritenhouse / Ridenhouse, recipient of a cow and bed.
- William W. Willison, executor.
- Jacob Geffs and Roger S. Davis, witnesses.

John H. remains supported by the 1883 Perrin biography and compiled Harry C. Partlow / FamilySearch material, not by this will.

### Lawful Heirs

The will refers to **all my lawful heirs** collectively but does not name them. The important clause is that Jacob had already given all lawful heirs **fifty dollars apiece**. This corrects the earlier snippet report, which had read the amount as $40.

Interpretation: Jacob recognized a broader lawful-heir class, but the will-book entry does not identify that class by name. Any estate packet, settlement, receipt, or distribution paper is now the best chance to identify those heirs.

### Polly / Mary

The will confirms the wife as **Polly**. It does not use the name Mary. Polly remains probably Mary / Polly Abrams Partlow by correlation with the indexed 1815 Indiana marriage and the 1860 household, but the will itself says only **Polly my wife**.

### William

The will names **William** as residuary beneficiary but does not give his surname, middle name, or relationship. William Franklin Partlow remains the leading identification by correlation with Jacob's 1860 household and derivative memorial evidence, but the will itself does not say "William Franklin Partlow" or "my son William."

### Polly Ritenhouse / Ridenhouse

The will gives **Polly Ritenhouse / Ridenhouse** a cow and a bed. Her relationship to Jacob is not stated. She is separate from wife Polly because the wife receives a life estate and this later clause carves out a specific bequest from William's residue.

Next target: identify Polly Ritenhouse / Ridenhouse in 1860-1880 Clark County records and test whether she was kin, ward, servant, granddaughter, step-kin, or unrelated.

### Witnesses and Executor

The witnesses were **Jacob Geffs** and **Roger S. Davis**. The executor was **William W. Willison**. The will does not identify any of them as kin. Their value is now FAN-club context: search Clark County deeds, tax lists, probate records, and census neighborhoods for proximity to Jacob Partlow, William Partlow, or Polly Ritenhouse / Ridenhouse.

## Evidence Assessment

This is an official Clark County will-book copy of Jacob Partlow's will and probate attestation. It is stronger than the prior derivative snippet trail and confirms the basic will details. It does not prove John H. Partlow as Jacob's son, does not identify Jacob's parents or birthplace, and does not repair the contradicted **Jacob -> Benjamin Partlow** link.

The will materially improves the research position by showing that Jacob had a broader lawful-heir class and that those heirs had received prior payments. The missing evidence is not the will text itself; it is any accompanying probate packet or settlement papers that may name the recipients.

The probate-index follow-up improves the control map by confirming that Jacob appears in the Clark County estate-paper index, but it does not supply a packet number that has been found online. It adds `(CC) 166` as the best new courthouse / court-book pointer.
