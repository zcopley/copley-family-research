## House and Marion Data Points

The PDF is a family-authored addendum about [[Places/862 Arlington Avenue Berkeley|the Arlington Avenue house]] and [[People/Marion Elizabeth Partlow|Marion Elizabeth Partlow Copley]]. It adds these usable data points:

- The house at 862 Arlington Avenue, at the corner of Arlington Avenue and Mendocino Street in [[Places/Berkeley California|Berkeley]], became a central family home for descendants of [[People/Michael Joseph Copley|Michael Joseph Copley]].
- The house was reportedly built just before World War I for Minnie Clough and her mother after they moved from Detroit to Berkeley.
- Minnie Clough's father had been involved in a successful Detroit piano company, and the PDF says the family inheritance helped fund the house.
- The house was designed by Berkeley architect Horace Simpson and described in family memory as resembling a French country home.
- Minnie Clough is described as founder of the first Montessori school in Berkeley.
- The house passed through later owners including an insurance agent and Monroe Deutsch, Vice President and Provost of UC Berkeley, before Michael Joseph Copley bought it in 1947.
- Monroe Deutsch is noted in the PDF as a civil-rights advocate who opposed Japanese American internment during World War II.
- Michael Joseph Copley and his family occupied the house from 1947 until his death in 1988.
- After Michael's estate, [[People/Thomas Partlow Copley|Tom Copley]] bought out [[People/Stephen Michael Copley|Steve Copley's]] interest and lived there with his family until 2002.
- The house later passed to Tom's children and their spouses, then to [[People/Erin E. Copley Bird|Erin Copley Bird]] and Bryan Bird.
- Marion met Michael at the University of Illinois, Urbana, while Michael was an assistant professor of chemistry and Marion was preparing to become an English teacher.
- Marion grew up on a farm in Casey, Illinois, retained a lifelong interest in gardening and agriculture, and actively managed inherited farmland.
- Marion advocated for preserving the Tilden Botanical Garden when local administrators considered abandoning it.
- Marion wrote a weekly column on California native plants for the `Oakland Tribune`.
- Marion wrote novels and poems; some were published, but many remained unpublished family papers.

## Photo Data Point

Tom's `Mom's photo` email identifies the attached image as a snapshot of his mother, another woman identified as Dee's grandmother, and Michael Joseph Copley partly visible in the corner. Tom thought the occasion was a LaForce Christmas party, probably in 1967-1968. A later same-day correction identifies Dee's grandmother as **[[People/Marian Agnes Hewetson|Marian Agnes Hewetson]]**, known as **Grammy**, Maggie's mother, and the woman seated between Marion and Michael. Tom noted the spelling distinction: Dee's grandmother was **Marian** with an `a`, while his mother was **Marion** with an `o`. This is useful as a family-photo identification lead, but the exact event date and location remain unverified.

## Origin-Research Leads

The Ivo / Talvas email chain contains AI-derived suggestions and Tom's reactions. These should be treated as prompts for future source checking, not as historical conclusions.

- One AI result suggested there may be no connection between an early Yorkshire Ivo associated with the Copley name and the Talvas family of the House of Belleme / Belleme tradition in France.
- The same AI output framed Ivo as a land speculator who acquired or developed land around the village of Copley, after which descendants adopted the place-name surname.
- Steve responded skeptically to the flattering AI characterization of the Copley family and separately noted that he was drafting an `Addendum 2` article titled `Christopher Copley Redux`.
- Tom listed four early English Copleys in Ireland as possible ancestral routes for the Roscommon Copleys: Christopher, Captain John, Henry, and Anthony. He described Christopher as from the original West Yorkshire root, and the latter three as related to the London cadet / Bredon-related branches.
- Tom also reported a weak AI suggestion that the Roscommon Copley name might be an Anglicization of a Gaelic name rendered `Kopsi`, which would represent a non-English surname-origin hypothesis.

## Evidence Caution

Use the house / Marion PDF as family testimony and near-primary family memory. Use the Ivo / Talvas / Kopsi material only as a research-lead list until the underlying medieval, linguistic, heraldic, or Irish records are identified and checked.
