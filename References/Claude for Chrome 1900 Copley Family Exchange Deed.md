# Claude for Chrome 1900 Copley Family Exchange Deed

This note identifies the previously unresolved 1900 FamilySearch legal-instrument lead from [[References/Claude for Chrome Copley Heirs Oil Title Search|the Copley heirs oil-title search]].

[Download the raw Claude report](../static/sources/claude-oil-title/1900-michael-copley-legal-instrument-6x4n-dwwy-claude-output.txt)

## Main Result

FamilySearch record [6X4N-DWWY](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:6X4N-DWWY) is a **family land-exchange deed**, not the South Penn Oil Company lease, an administrator's deed, or a probate record.

The deed was executed **22 August 1900** and recorded **13 October 1900**. The parties were:

- **Ann Copley**
- daughters **Margaret Copley**, **Ann Copley**, **Sarah Copley**, and **Bridget Gillooly**
- Bridget's husband **Patrick Gillooly**
- grantee **John Copley**

Michael Copley was already deceased and appears as a source of title, not as a living party.

## Property Exchange

The deed recites an exchange involving:

- a **55-acre tract** on **Bungport Run of Cove Lick**, associated with Michael Copley's will;
- **27½ acres of John Copley's one-half interest** in a **200-acre Sand Fork tract** previously associated with Michael and John Copley;
- an earlier deed dated **1 May 1878**, with the book designation read in the report as `Deed Book No. 1, page 321`;
- a final metes-and-bounds description containing **79 acres and 48 poles**, more or less.

The precise relationship between the recited 55 acres and the final 79-acre / 48-pole description remains unclear from the report transcription. The older deed-book designation also needs image-level confirmation.

## Oil-Title Relevance

The deed contains **no oil, gas, mineral-rights, royalty, lease, or South Penn language**. It therefore does not identify the lessors or terms of the separate **Michael Copley Heirs 128-Acre Lease**.

It is still highly relevant because it documents a family title rearrangement affecting Cove Lick and Sand Fork land immediately before South Penn completed **Michael Copley Heirs well No. 1** on **13 September 1900**. The timing makes it a key title-chain record, but the reason for the exchange and its relationship to the oil lease remain unproven.

The deed should **not** be interpreted as consolidating the Sand Fork tract in John's name. Its recital says the family exchange involved **John's 27½-acre Sand Fork interest** as consideration for the interest conveyed to him.
