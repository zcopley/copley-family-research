Claude for Chrome Lewis County Personal Property Tax Check

This note preserves Zach’s Claude for Chrome handoff for the Ancestry collection Virginia, U.S., Personal Property Tax Records, 1782-1900, focused on Lewis County Murray / Murry / Murrey entries before the 1860 census.

Main result: The check found image-verified pre-1860 Lewis County Murray / Murry / Murrow tax rows beginning with Murray James and Murry Patrick in the same confirmed 1854 sequence. Follow-up image verification confirms Patrick on page 16, line 14, written Murry Patrick, with one white male tithable and one horse valued at $50, and confirms James on page 15, line 24, written Murray James, with one white male tithable and no listed taxable property. The 1855-1860 gap pass then confirms Murray James and Murray Patrick again in 1857 under Geo. J. Marsh, confirms Murray James again in 1860, and adds Murray / Murrow Hudson, Murray Edwards, and a separate-district Murray William as new FAN-club tax clues. No Luke, Lucas, or Margaret Murray / Murry appears in the checked or accessible M-sections through 1860. A native-tile check of the reported 1853 Eliza Jane row on i3811884-00262 found that the name is written Merry, Eliza Jane, not Murry; keep it as a potentially relevant variant / neighbor clue, not as confirmed Murry evidence. The May 2026 i3811884-00362 proof / summary page check confirms the James / Patrick list year as 1854 by a legible handwritten 4 after preprinted 185. Taxable-persons page i3811884-00361 reads C. Kester, and Patrick’s own page i3811884-00379 carries faint handwriting strongly consistent with the same C. Kester district label. No signed commissioner oath or full commissioner identification has been found.

Evidence Caution

This is a browser research handoff, not a captured primary-source transcription from images preserved in the wiki. The Ancestry image URLs and line references should be revisited and the original images should be captured or independently transcribed before any claim is upgraded to verified.

The original May 2026 handoff had a scope inconsistency: the header said 1817-1856 were checked, but the year table said 1856 was not reached and the negative summary said the latest checked year was 1855, partially. The later 1855-1860 gap pass resolves much of that uncertainty but still leaves important coverage caveats: 1855 M-section name pages appear absent from the digitized set, 1856 is only partly checked because the John Kee / Lee district M-section was not reached, 1858 is partial because Geo. J. Marsh stops at L and E. Slesher has only a short M page, 1859 is partial, and the 1860 Elijah Flesher 1st District name pages are essentially absent before the end card.

The Patrick Murry and James Murray image-verification follow-ups improve the evidence level for those specific rows, but the original images themselves are not yet stored in the repo. Treat the names, pages, lines, and tax-column reads as image-verified by Claude for Chrome. The year is now confirmed as 1854 by proof / summary image i3811884-00362. The best current district / commissioner-label clue is C. Kester, read from taxable-persons page i3811884-00361 and confirmed by a native-tile reread. Neither i3811884-00360, the bottom/signature recheck of i3811884-00362, the James-page header check on i3811884-00377, nor the intervening proof-page check on i3811884-00378 corroborated it. The later Patrick-page check on i3811884-00379 does materially strengthen the label assignment for Patrick: its own District of field contains faint handwriting strongly consistent with C. Kester. Image i3811884-00380 is a neutral proof-page follow-up: effectively unreadable in the district field, no signature or oath, and no contradiction. Because the 00379 read is not an unambiguous independent letter-by-letter confirmation, and because no signed oath has been found, keep the label caveated.

RQ-M1 Interpretation

The tax-list result strengthens the post-1850 arrival model for the Ireland-born Murry group:

  • No Murray / Murry / Murrey tax evidence was found in the checked 1817-1850 span.
  • The reported 1853 Eliza Jane row on i3811884-00262 is image-read as Merry, Eliza Jane, not Murry.
  • Patrick Murry and James Murray appear in the same confirmed-1854 tax-list sequence and both reappear in 1857 under Geo. J. Marsh; James also appears in 1860.
  • John Loghan and Owen Loghan appear as taxable Lewis County men in the 1854 context, with 00320 directly confirming 1854 for their folded return; 00321 continues the same Ree district into an M/Mc page and strengthens the conservative John C. Ree / John Cs. Ree reading; 00322 directly confirms 1854 for the proof / abstract paired to 00321; 00323 continues the same Ree district into M/N names; 00324 is another 1854 proof / abstract for the Ree district pages; 00325 continues the same Ree district into N/O names; 00326 is another 1854 proof / abstract, most likely paired to 00325 by page-sequence pattern; 00327 continues the Ree district into P/R-type names; 00328 is the paired 1854 proof / abstract page likely closing the P/R list; 00329 continues the same John Ree district into R/S names; and 00330 is the paired 1854 proof / abstract for 00329. This remains a different district from the C. Kester district tied to Patrick and James.
  • Luke Murray married Anne Loghan in Lewis County in 1854, but no taxable Luke was found before or during the checked 1854 M-section pages.

This makes the older Luke & Margaret Murry parent-couple problem sharper. Patrick’s 1871 death register names parents Luke and Margaret Murry, but no older taxable Luke has appeared in Lewis County tax lists through the checked or accessible 1817-1860 M-sections. If the tax-list pass is complete enough, the older Luke may have died before the family’s Lewis County tax appearance, remained in Ireland, settled elsewhere, or left only church / probate / deed traces outside the pages checked.

The result does not prove a pre-1843 Murray anchor family. With the Eliza Jane row corrected to Merry, the first confirmed Murray / Murry tax appearances in this pass remain James and Patrick in 1854, while the broader arrival window remains post-1850 unless earlier records surface.
