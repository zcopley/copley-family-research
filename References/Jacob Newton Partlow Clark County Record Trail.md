# Jacob Newton Partlow Clark County Record Trail

This note preserves the June 2026 web-accessible record trail for **Jacob Partlow / Jacob Newton Partlow** of Indiana and Clark County, Illinois. It follows the corrective Rappahannock chancery result: Jacob should no longer be treated as a proved or likely child of Benjamin Partlow unless a new primary record explains his omission from Benjamin's complete heir file.

## Mary Halleck vs. Mary / Polly Abrams

The first record-trail pass favored **Mary / Polly Abrams** because the Indiana marriage index is the strongest direct marriage record located so far.

The Indiana marriage index records **Polly Abrams** marrying **Jacob Partlow** on **13 Sep 1815**. "Polly" was a common period nickname for Mary. The 1860 census places Jacob with **Mary Partlow**, both age 67 and both born Kentucky. This first pass did not retrieve an original Mary Halleck marriage record.

Later follow-up reported a two-marriage compiled-source structure: **Mary Halleck** as Jacob's first wife and mother of John Halleck Partlow, followed by **Mary / Polly Abrams** as Jacob's later wife, named in the 1815 Indiana marriage index and apparently in the 1867 will as Polly / Mary. Treat that as a working hypothesis. The Polly Abrams marriage has indexed-record support; the Mary Halleck marriage still needs original or near-original corroboration.

## Jacob's Parents

No primary record in this pass names Jacob's parents.

The Find a Grave memorial's father field lists Benjamin Partlow, but this is a derivative user-created claim. It is outweighed for now by [[References/Rappahannock Partlow Chancery Case 1842-002]], where Benjamin Partlow's complete 69-image heir and estate-distribution file names eight child / heir lines and omits Jacob / Jacob Newton / Nute Partlow throughout.

## John H. Partlow as Jacob's Son

No primary record in this pass directly says "John H. Partlow, son of Jacob." The relationship is currently supported by:

- the 1883 Perrin county-history biography saying John H. Partlow was a son of Jacob Partlow;
- John H.'s 1840 and 1850 Clark County presence;
- Jacob's 1860 Johnson Township presence;
- family and compiled-source continuity.

That is enough to keep **John H. -> Jacob** as a strong working conclusion, but the highest-value proof target is still Jacob's probate / estate file.
