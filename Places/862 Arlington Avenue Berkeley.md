# 862 Arlington Avenue, Berkeley

## Overview

The house at 862 Arlington Avenue, at the corner of Arlington Avenue and Mendocino Street in [[Places/Berkeley California|Berkeley]], was the major California family home of [[People/Michael Joseph Copley|Michael Joseph Copley]] and [[People/Marion Elizabeth Partlow|Marion Elizabeth Partlow Copley]]. Family memory says many of Michael and Marion's children and grandchildren either grew up in the house or spent meaningful time there.

## House History

The June 2026 family-authored PDF says the house was built shortly before World War I for Minnie Clough and her mother, who moved to Berkeley after the death of Minnie's father. The note says the Clough family wealth came from a successful Detroit piano company and helped fund the house.

The same note identifies the architect as Berkeley architect **Horace Simpson** and describes the house as having qualities of a French country home. It also says Minnie Clough founded the first Montessori school in Berkeley and owned the house until 1925, when she retired to Carmel.

The reported ownership chain is:

| Period | Owner / Occupant | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| pre-World War I-1925 | Minnie Clough and her mother | Original family owners; Montessori-school connection. |
| after 1925 | unnamed insurance agent | Transitional owner named only by occupation in the family PDF. |
| before 1947 | Monroe Deutsch | Vice President and Provost of UC Berkeley; remembered in the PDF as a civil-rights advocate opposed to Japanese American internment. |
| 1947-1988 | [[People/Michael Joseph Copley|Michael Joseph Copley]] and family | Michael bought the house after becoming Director of the USDA Western Regional Research Laboratory in [[Places/Albany California|Albany]]. |
| 1988-2002 | [[People/Thomas Partlow Copley|Thomas Partlow Copley]] and family | Tom bought out [[People/Stephen Michael Copley|Stephen Copley]]'s estate interest and lived there with his family. |
| after 2002 | Tom's children and spouses, then [[People/Erin E. Copley Bird|Erin Copley Bird]] and Bryan Bird | Later family ownership path described in the PDF. |

## Family Significance

The house marks the domestic side of the postwar California branch. It connects Michael's USDA Albany appointment, Marion's gardening and writing life, Stephen and Tom's Berkeley childhoods, and the later Tom-line family home.

For Marion, the house was the setting for gardening, entertaining, family education, and household life during Michael's WRRL leadership years. For Tom's generation, it became both a childhood home and, later, the house he returned to after Michael's final years and estate settlement.
